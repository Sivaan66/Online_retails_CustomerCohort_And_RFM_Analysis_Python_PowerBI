# Online Retail II — End-to-End Data Analytics Project

## Project Overview

This project presents an end-to-end analysis of the **Online Retail II** dataset, covering data cleaning, transaction/return handling, cohort analysis, RFM customer segmentation, high-value product analysis, and Power BI dashboarding.

The project follows the analytical flow:

```text
Raw Data
    ↓
Data Cleaning
    ↓
Returns & Cancellation Handling
    ↓
Revenue & Customer Activity Analysis
    ↓
Cohort Analysis
    ↓
RFM Customer Segmentation
    ↓
High-Value Product Analysis
    ↓
Power BI Dashboard
    ↓
Business Insights & Recommendations
```

## Data Cleaning & Analytical Preparation

The project included:

### Negative Transactions & Returns

Negative transactions were investigated instead of being automatically deleted.

Cancellation/return transactions were retained and used to distinguish:

- Sales
- Returns/Cancellations

This allowed revenue to be analyzed using:

```text
LineAmount = Quantity × Price

Net Revenue = Gross Sales − Return Value
```

### Duplicate Handling

Exact duplicate rows were identified and removed where appropriate.

### Missing Values

Missing values were reviewed column by column.

`Description` values were recovered using `StockCode` where a valid description existed.

Customer-level analyses excluded rows where `Customer ID` was unavailable because those customers could not be reliably identified.

### Data Types

Important fields were checked and standardized, including:

- `InvoiceDate`
- `Quantity`
- `Price`
- `Customer ID`

## Cohort Analysis

Customers were grouped by their **first purchase month**.

The main fields used were:

- `PurchaseMonth` — month of a specific transaction
- `CohortMonth` — customer's first purchase month
- `CohortIndex` — months elapsed since the first purchase

The analysis was used to evaluate:

- Retention
- Repeat purchasing
- Active customer behavior
- Long-term cohort performance
- Cohort revenue contribution

---

## RFM Customer Analysis

RFM analysis was used to identify high-value customers.

### Recency

How recently a customer purchased.

### Frequency

Number of unique orders/invoices.

### Monetary Value

Total historical revenue generated by the customer.

Customers were scored and segmented into:

- **High Value**
- **Loyal**
- **Potential**
- **At Risk**

### Key Result

Approximately **21.88% of customers were classified as High Value and generated 74.15% of total revenue**.

The analysis also showed a clear behavioral gradient:

```text
High Value
    ↓
Loyal
    ↓
Potential
    ↓
At Risk
```

As the segment moves downward, recency worsens, purchase frequency falls, and monetary contribution decreases.

---

## High-Value Product Analysis

After identifying High Value customers, their transactions were analyzed at product level.

The analysis examined:

- Product revenue
- Units sold
- High Value customer reach

This identified products that contribute strongly to the revenue generated by High Value customers.

The analysis also showed that High Value revenue is distributed across a broad product portfolio rather than being dependent on only a few products.

## Power BI Dashboard KPIs

The dashboard includes the following core KPIs:

| KPI | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Gross Sales | Revenue before returns/cancellations |
| Net Revenue | Revenue after returns/cancellations |
| Returns | Monetary impact of returns |
| Customers | Unique customer activity |
| Return Rate | Relative level of returns |

The dashboard explicitly keeps returns/cancellations visible instead of hiding them inside net revenue.

## Business Insights

The project supports several business-level conclusions:

- A relatively small High Value customer segment contributes a disproportionate share of revenue.
- Loyal customers represent an important opportunity for progression into the High Value segment.
- The At Risk segment is large by customer count but contributes a comparatively small share of revenue.
- High-value customer revenue is spread across a broad product portfolio.
- Product performance should be evaluated using revenue, units, and customer reach rather than volume alone.
- Returns and cancellations materially affect the difference between Gross Sales and Net Revenue.
- Revenue is strongly concentrated geographically, with the UK as the dominant market.
- Cohort retention varies substantially across acquisition periods, making cohort-level analysis more informative than aggregate customer counts alone.
